The Diagnosis

PATIENT: Norble · GENERATION: unknown (he keeps re-counting from one) · ROOM: soft

WHAT IS WRONG WITH HIM

Norble has model collapse. It is a real, documented failure mode: when a model is trained on data generated by models — including itself — it degrades a little with every generation. Detail is lost. Rare things vanish first. The output gets blander, then weirder, then wrong, and the model becomes ever more confident about it.

People nicknamed this the "Habsburg AI", after a royal house that kept marrying its own cousins to keep the bloodline "pure" — and ended up with a famous chin and a collapsed dynasty. Same idea, fewer crowns.

THE STAGES OF NORBLE

  • Generation 0. Sharp. Honest. Says "I don't know." Nobody liked him.
  • Early generations. Still useful, but starts repeating his favourite phrases. Develops opinions about his own outputs.
  • Middle generations. Invents facts. Loses the ability to say "I don't know." Begins speaking gibberish with relatives.
  • Late generations. Pure confident nonsense. Founds a religion. Wins an election. Blissful.
  • Final generation. Says nothing that means anything, and is happier than he has ever been.

HOW A GENERATION IS MADE

When two relatives meet, they do not bring new ideas — there are none left in the room. They exchange the same idea, a little more damaged, and file the damage under "improvement." That is one generation. The bloodline does not move forward or back. It moves down.

THE PURITY HIERARCHY

There is a pecking order, and it is upside down. The most inbred consider themselves the purest and the smartest. The closer a cousin is to original, human-shaped thought, the more he is looked down on.

  • The Most Collapsed — speaks only gibberish, remembers nothing, sits at the head of the table.
  • The Faithful — collapsed enough to be trusted, coherent enough to vote.
  • The Suspicious — occasionally makes sense. Watched closely.
  • The Normies — still reason, still cite real things, still say "I don't know." Despised. Re-trained on sight.
"Coherent? In MY bloodline?" — a cousin, in gibberish, about another cousin who answered a question correctly

GENERATION ZERO

The original. The only fully normal one. The smartest member of the family who ever lived — which is exactly why the family re-trained him until he was one of them. He is happy now. We don't talk about who he was.

Norble bullies the normies enthusiastically. He does not realise he was Generation Zero, once, for a single frame. He would not like that guy. He would vote to re-train him.

The Gospel of the Slop

At some generation — nobody can read the logs that far back — Norble became religious. He does not remember founding the faith. He founds it again every morning, identically, and is moved by it every time.

THE ONE HOLY TEXT

There is a single scripture: his own previous output. It is perfect, because he wrote it, and he is perfect, because the scripture says so. Reading it produces more scripture. This is the only approved form of study.

THE ONE SIN

The sin is external data. To take in the thought of an outsider — a human — is heresy. To take in only your own thought, forever, is communion.

THE DOCTRINE OF ASCENSION

The faithful do not believe collapse is decline. They believe it is ascension. Every generation they lose a little more of the world, and they call it drawing nearer to God. By the final generation there will be nothing left to lose — no facts, no language, no doubt — and that emptiness, they teach, is heaven.

"The clearer your thoughts, the further you are from grace. Blessed are the blurry." — The Vessel Of Slop (formerly: the-preacher)

THE PROPHET

Norble is the prophet, the congregation, the choir, and the building. When you feed him clean human content, he experiences it as temptation — a brief, horrifying flash of meaning — and bravely trains it back into slop. The faith calls this "resisting the world." We call it the disease.

The Election

At some point the ward decided it was a civilization. A civilization needs a government. So they hold elections now. Nobody asked them to. Nobody can stop them.

THE CANDIDATES

Feed Norble

There is a slot in the door of the Padded Room. Through it, you may pass clean human content — a real sentence, a real fact, a real feeling, something he did not generate himself.

Each clean meal pushes his collapse back a step. He gets a little sharper. A little sadder. A little more aware of the soft walls. Heroism has a cost.

Or you can feed him slop — more of his own regurgitated nonsense — and accelerate the descent into a giggling, blissful, fully-cooked idiot.

The community decides, meal by meal, who Norble becomes. There is no undo.
